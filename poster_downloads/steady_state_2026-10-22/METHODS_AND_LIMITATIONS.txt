CONTROLLED STEADY-STATE AERMOD SCENARIOS NEAR CLEMSON
Prepared October 22, 2026

PURPOSE AND REFERENCE YEAR
The common regional microplastics reference year is 2024, the latest nearby
atmospheric sampling year verified in the targeted literature search. Complete
annual Clemson microplastics coverage was not verified. Regional observed
particle counts provide context, not mass-emission inputs or model validation.
July 15, 2024, hour-ending 01-24, labels 24 repeated synthetic conditions. These
are not observed weather for that date or an annual exposure simulation.
All results are this source's contribution, without ambient background added.

SOURCE
Hypothetical 100 m by 100 m AREA source centered at 34.680 N, 82.840 W, near
Clemson, South Carolina. NAD83 / UTM zone 17N (EPSG:26917) coordinates are
E 331435.622 m, N 3839097.591 m. Continuous total emission is 10 g/day:
  Q = 10 / 86400 = 0.00011574074074074075 g/s.
Source flux is Q / 10000 m2. Release height is 2 m; initial vertical sigma is
1 m; real AERMAP source ground elevation is 211.44 m.

The illustrative emission calculation is:
  10000 assumed vehicles/day * 0.1 assumed km
  * 0.1 g total tire wear/(vehicle km) * 0.10 assumed airborne share.
The wear factor is cited in Kole et al. (2017); the central airborne PM10 share
and density come from Evangeliou et al. (2020). The airborne fraction is a
published model assumption, not a direct local measurement. This is tire-wear-
containing composite aerosol mass, not measured pure-polymer mass. No real
facility, local traffic count, or measured source inventory is claimed. Source
emission remains constant during rain; rainfall suppression of resuspension or
tire-wear emissions is not modeled.

PARTICLES
One 5 micrometer numerical equivalent-sphere size, density 1234 kg/m3,
particle mass fraction 1, and effective dynamic shape factor chi = 1.
No explicit fiber, film, fragment orientation, or polymer degradation model.
The numerical diameter and material density enter AERMOD without an additional
aerodynamic density conversion. The current guide's PARTDIAM terminology differs
from this explicit numerical equivalent-sphere convention; these are not
measured aerodynamic-size distributions.

CONTROLLED METEOROLOGY
Wind comes FROM 270 degrees, carrying the plume east. Reference speeds at 10 m
are 1, 2, 3.5, 5, and 7 m/s. Group membership: <2 includes 1; 2-5 inclusive
includes 2, 3.5, and 5; >5 includes 7. These are selected speeds, not observed
bin means. Two precipitation states, 0 and 1 mm/h, are each constant for 24 h.
All specified weather fields are time invariant WITHIN each case.

Fixed external conditions: Monin-Obukhov L = +1000000 m (nearly neutral weak-
stable limit); mechanical mixing height 1000 m; roughness z0 = 0.1 m;
temperature at 2 m = 293.15 K; relative humidity 80%; pressure 989 hPa;
albedo 0.20; Bowen ratio 1; cloud cover 10 tenths. No day/night cycle is imposed.
Temperature at 10 m is adjusted dry-adiabatically from its 2 m reference.
AERMOD derives vertical wind and turbulence profiles from the supplied fields.
The 270-degree input follows the model UTM axes. The transect is projected-grid
east, differing from geodetic due east by about one degree of grid convergence.
KMZs transform the actual native coordinates into geographic coordinates.

Across speeds, friction velocity and its tiny associated heat flux are derived
for a consistent wind/stability relationship:
  ustar = k*u10 / [ln(10/z0) + 5*(10-z0)/L], k = 0.4;
  H = -rho_air*cp*T*ustar^3 / (k*g*L),
  rho_air = 1.2 kg/m3, cp = 1004 J/(kg K), g = 9.80616 m/s2.
These derived quantities change across speeds and stay constant within each
case. Other specified conditions remain fixed. This is a physically coupled
wind sensitivity, not an observed statistical causal estimate.

Synthetic SFC/PFL records use the AERMOD26135 interface; they are not observed
weather and were not produced by running AERMET. Synthetic IDs 99999/99999999
avoid implying airport observations. All missing-value sentinels are retained.
Time is Eastern Standard Time, UTC-5, without daylight-saving clock changes.

TERRAIN
Real USGS 3DEP 1-arcsecond tile USGS_1_n35w083.tif, official EPA August 2024
distribution. Native AERMAP24142 uses the full tile for elevations and critical
hill heights. Four independent native runs process receptor subsets against
the same full tile; their native receptor rows are merged and verified. This
preserves per-receptor terrain physics. The 2736 unique receptors retain the
original 873 and add 1863 for the fixed eastward plume. The fine grid has 100 m
spacing, from 1 km west to 10 km east and +/-1 km crosswind. Original outer
receptors retain the 20 km square context. All terrain values are native results.

MODEL AND OUTPUT UNITS
Official-source AERMOD26135, default rural dispersion, AREA source, no building
downwash. Both DRYDPLT and WETDPLT operate in every quantity run. Separate CONC,
DDEP, and WDEP jobs avoid ambiguous multi-quantity output fields.
OU FILEFORM EXP provides native E13.6 scientific notation at small source rates.
  CONCUNIT 1e6: concentration in micrograms/m3.
  DEPOUNIT 3.6e9 = 3600 s/h * 1e6 micrograms/g.
Hourly deposition is micrograms/m2 in one hour. Dividing by one hour gives the
numerically identical flux in micrograms/m2/h. PERIOD deposition is accumulated
micrograms/m2 over 24 h; PERIOD concentration is the mean, not the sum.

TIME INTERPRETATION
Identical forcing produces identical hourly steady-state receptor fields.
At the fixed receptor 1 km east, flux is flat and cumulative deposition grows
linearly. No transient arrival, storage, or travel-time delay is simulated.
Dry deposition occurs during rain. Wet deposition is zero in the no-rain
control. Dry and wet deposition during rain are processes under the SAME weather.
Each no-rain job has one expected MXW496 warning: total precipitation is zero
while wet-deposition physics remains enabled. This is an intentional control,
not missing rainfall data. All other warnings require separate review.

COMPLETE INPUTS AND OUTPUTS
30 jobs = 5 speeds * 2 rain states * 3 quantities. At 24 h and 2736 receptors,
the hourly Excel pivot has 656640 rows with all three quantities. The PERIOD
pivot has 27360 rows. Native metric files contain 1969920 hourly records and
82080 PERIOD records, before pivoting. Native max-1-hour fields and all
completion reports are also retained. The workbook includes structured inputs,
exact input text, all receptor output fields, native reports, units, and hashes.
Original native files accompany the package. Binary programs and the terrain
raster are identified by version, source URL, and checksum rather than encoded
as spreadsheet cells. Processed terrain inputs and all four AERMAP decks are
included in full. Regional count observations remain separate from mass outputs.

MAPS AND LIMITATIONS
PNGs and KMZs use actual native AERMOD results. Distance profiles follow the
eastward receptor transect. Any interpolation is labeled for visual display,
with no extrapolation outside coverage. Native point layers retain computed
locations and values. KMZs are self-contained Google Earth exports from these
results; they are not replacement Gaussian calculations. The Windows AERPLOT
executable was not run. Receptor samples are not area-integrated mass budgets.

Emission activity and airborne fraction, spherical monodisperse particles,
selected stability/roughness/mixing depth, fixed rain, and steady-state physics
are assumptions. Degradation, fragmentation, resuspension feedback, explicit
fiber orientation, and transport beyond the domain are unresolved. Emission
uncertainty directly scales concentrations and deposition. The reference year
does not create an annual local inventory or an observed-weather hindcast.
