DATA DESCRIPTION: particle_distance_profiles_v2
Revision: 2026-10-20
Project: Illustrative Clemson atmospheric microplastic transport and deposition

WHAT THIS SPECIFIC FIGURE SHOWS
The upper row compares nominal equivalent-sphere diameters of 0.5, 1, 5,
and 10 micrometers, all at material density 1.05 g/cm3 (1050 kg/m3).
The lower row retains the original density comparison: 0.90, 1.05, and
1.40 g/cm3 at diameter 10 micrometers. Each row shows concentration,
accumulated dry deposition, and accumulated wet deposition by distance.
These are hypothetical source-normalized model results, not measurements of
ambient microplastic abundance or estimates of a real source's emissions.

PARTICLES AND SHAPE FACTOR
Every AERMOD case is a monodisperse, equivalent spherical particle surrogate,
with particle mass fraction 1.0. The corresponding dynamic shape factor is
chi = 1. No fibers, films, fragments, foam, orientation distributions, or
explicit nonspherical drag are represented in these dispersion runs.
Chi is a dynamic SHAPE factor, not a size factor. Separate settling figures
explore chi > 1 analytically; they do not change the AERMOD distance profiles.
Density values are sensitivity assumptions, not polymer identifications.
Equal mass emission across cases implies different emitted particle counts.

Diameter convention: these numerical diameters are supplied directly to
AERMOD26135 PARTDIAM together with actual material density in PARTDENS.
The model's implemented settling calculation uses both diameter and density.
The current guide calls PARTDIAM a mass-mean aerodynamic diameter; this
project explicitly uses a numerical equivalent-sphere surrogate convention.
No additional density conversion to aerodynamic diameter was applied. The
figure must not be described as observations of measured aerodynamic sizes.

LOCATION, SOURCE, AND TERRAIN
Hypothetical source center: 34.6800 degrees N, 82.8400 degrees W, near Clemson
in upstate South Carolina. Projected center: E 331435.622 m,
N 3839097.591 m, NAD83 / UTM zone 17N (EPSG:26917).
Source type: square AREA, 100 m x 100 m, centered at that coordinate;
southwest vertex E 331385.622 m, N 3839047.591 m.
Continuous assumed total emission: 1.0 g/s, equivalent to 0.0001 g/(s m2).
The native AERMAP include rounds the southwest vertex to
E 331385.62 m, N 3839047.59 m. This centimeter rounding is shared by cases.
Release height: 2 m above local ground; initial vertical sigma: 1 m.
AERMAP source ground elevation: 211.44 m above mean sea level.
Rural dispersion option; no building downwash or measured source inventory.

Terrain is the actual USGS 3DEP 1-arcsecond (approximately 30 m) tile
USGS_1_n35w083.tif, from the official EPA August 2024 distribution:
https://gaftp.epa.gov/Air/aqmg/3dep/08-2024/1_arcsecond/USGS_1_n35w083.zip
AERMAP24142 processed the full tile for terrain and critical hill heights.
There are 873 unique ground-level receptors: a 100 m-spaced inner square
within +/-1 km and a 1 km-spaced outer square within +/-10 km, merged with
duplicate coordinates removed. Receptor ground elevations span
187.07-301.27 m. Square-domain corners extend to approximately 14.14 km
radial distance. This is a local 20 km x 20 km study domain.

DATE RANGE AND ACTUAL METEOROLOGICAL CONDITIONS
Requested period: 2023-01-01 hour-ending 01 through 2023-12-31 hour-ending 24.
All model hours use Eastern Standard Time (UTC-5), with no daylight-saving
clock change. The request contains 8760 hours. AERMOD excludes 390 calm and
307 missing hours; results use 8063 valid noncalm modeled hours. These exact
counts come from model I440/I460 flags and summary counters, not inferred
from zero-valued concentrations.

The figure integrates the varying observed weather of the full year; it is
not a fixed wind-speed, fixed stability, or single precipitation event run.
Over the 8063 valid modeled hours, AERMET reference wind speed has mean
2.463 m/s, median 2.08 m/s, and range 0.39-12.21 m/s. There are 3849 hours
below 2 m/s, 3746 at 2 to less than 5 m/s, and 468 at or above 5 m/s.
Monin-Obukhov length is positive (stable) for 4719 hours and negative
(unstable) for 3344 hours. Wind direction varies hourly and is reported as
the direction the wind comes FROM. All particle cases use exactly the same
processed surface and upper-air files, including the same hourly wind,
mixing, stability, temperature, and precipitation inputs.

Surface observations: KCEU, Clemson-Oconee County Regional Airport,
USAF 723118 / WBAN 53850, latitude 34.672 N, longitude 82.881 W.
NOAA hourly ISD/ISHD data supply observed surface weather. NOAA one-minute
ASOS winds for all 12 months of 2023 are processed using AERMINUTE26135;
supported hourly observations remain available to AERMET as fallback.
Wind measurement height: 7.92 m. The 2023 raw weather records report
elevation 272.0 m; AERMOD PROFBASE is therefore 272 m. The station inventory
lists 270.8 m, a different metadata value that was not substituted for the
raw-record processing elevation.
Hourly data source:
https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/noaa/2023/723118-53850-2023.gz
One-minute data source pattern (MM = 01 through 12):
https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/automated-surface-observing-system-one-minute-pg1/access/2023/MM/asos-1min-pg1-KCEU-2023MM.dat

Upper-air observations: NOAA IGRA, FFC / Peachtree City, Georgia,
USM00072215 (AERMET station identifier 00072215), latitude 33.3558 N,
longitude 84.5672 W, elevation 244.9 m, approximately 217 km from the
hypothetical source. IGRA is used instead of obsolete FSL-format downloads.
No sounding exists for 2023-07-29 in the retrieved archive; none was invented.
https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/igra/data/data-por/USM00072215-data.txt.zip

AERMET26135 produces aermet/clemson.sfc and aermet/clemson.pfl.
Surface assumptions, held constant: albedo 0.20, Bowen ratio 1.0, roughness
length 0.10 m, one annual sector. These are illustrative and were not
derived using AERSURFACE or on-site turbulence observations. The AERMINUTE
IFW/sonic status is ASSUMED effective 2023-01-01 for processing; this is not
a verified KCEU instrument commissioning date. That uncertainty carries
through all plots and is documented in aerminute/ifw_clarification.json.

Precipitation is the observed, processed forcing, with dry and wet depletion
active in every run. An independent raw-record audit finds 26 unknown
precipitation hours in the calendar year. Some raw/processed categories
also disagree. Among model-valid hours, 24 are unknown or discordant under
the stricter weather-comparison audit; those hours remain part of these
native AERMOD period outputs when otherwise model-valid. AERMET may default
absent precipitation to zero. Therefore modeled zero rain is not always an
observed dry hour. The figure does not silently reprocess rainfall or claim
complete observed wet-removal coverage.

EXACT MODEL OUTPUTS USED AND WHAT WAS REUSED
Native EPA AERMOD26135 was executed separately for concentration, dry
deposition, and wet deposition, with DFAULT, RURAL, DRYDPLT and WETDPLT.
All outputs have identical source geometry, terrain, meteorology, calendar
window, and receptor coordinates. The plots use authentic PERIOD PLOTFILEs
named annual.plt (this conventional filename is not an extra averaging rule).

Size cases, relative to clemson_amp/:
  0.5 micrometers: aermod/d0p5_r1050/{metric}/annual.plt
  1 micrometer:   aermod/d01_r1050/{metric}/annual.plt
  5 micrometers:  aermod/d05_r1050/{metric}/annual.plt
  10 micrometers: aermod/d10_r1050/{metric}/annual.plt
Additional density cases at 10 micrometers:
  0.90 g/cm3: aermod/d10_r0900/{metric}/annual.plt
  1.40 g/cm3: aermod/d10_r1400/{metric}/annual.plt
Here {metric} is concentration, dry_deposition, or wet_deposition.
The original 1 and 10 micrometer and density outputs are reused unchanged.
The original project contained no 0.5 or 5 micrometer native outputs; these
two cases were newly run using the identical retained forcing and source.
No model curves were obtained by interpolating or relabeling other sizes.
The original 30 micrometer case is retained in the project but excluded
from the revised size row.

Concentration units: micrograms/m3, PERIOD arithmetic mean over valid
noncalm modeled hours. Dry and wet deposition units: g/m2, PERIOD
accumulated mass per area over the processed valid hours. Deposition curves
are NOT mean fluxes and NOT complete-calendar deposition estimates for
excluded hours. Both dry and wet removal can operate in the same hour.
Only PERIOD values are used. Optional 24-hour concentration statistics
produce completeness warnings, which do not supply data for this figure.

DISTANCE BINNING AND NUMERICAL PRECISION
For each receptor, radial distance is calculated from the source center
using UTM coordinates. Annulus boundaries are 0, 0.1, ... 1.0, 2, 3, ...
16 km. Bins include their lower edge and exclude their upper edge.
The plotted x coordinate is the arithmetic mean receptor radius in the bin,
not simply its midpoint. The plotted y coordinate is the unweighted
arithmetic mean of ALL receptor values in that bin, including printed
zeros. Different nested-grid spacings therefore contribute different sample
densities; no receptor-area or annulus-area weighting is applied.
Only positive bin means at positive mean radius can appear on logarithmic
axes. A bin with nonpositive mean radius would be retained in the CSV but
hidden on the logarithmic distance axis; the CSV records this flag.
Zero bin means are omitted as gaps, never replaced by an arbitrary floor.
The exported profile CSV records bin edges, actual mean radius, mean value,
receptor count, and zero counts for inspection.

AERMOD PLOTFILE values are printed to five decimal places. Printed zero
can represent a small positive value below the output precision. This
especially affects the smallest-particle wet-deposition fields. Annular
averaging does not recover subprecision native values: a very small mean
can arise from a few printed 0.00001 values and many printed zeros. Means
below about 0.000005 g/m2 can be dominated by rounding and do not imply
that finer physical resolution was achieved. Zero at output precision must
not be interpreted as proof of absent transport or deposition. Neither
directional averaging nor log-axis omission changes the native values.

INTERPRETATION AND REPRODUCIBILITY
These are direction-averaged receptor sample profiles under annual varying
weather, not instantaneous downwind plume trajectories, measured transport
distances, area-integrated mass recovery, or causal estimates of weather
effects. They do not simulate degradation, fragmentation, resuspension,
explicit fiber/film orientation, or transport outside this domain.
