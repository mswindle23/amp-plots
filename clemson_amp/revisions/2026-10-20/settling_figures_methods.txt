METHODS: theoretical_settling_v2 and settling_velocity_vs_chi
Revision: 2026-10-20

These are analytical calculations in air, separate from AERMOD outputs.
No source location, meteorological date range, or field observations are
used in the calculation. The associated AERMOD project is near Clemson,
South Carolina, and uses 2023 weather; its location and dates do not make
these constant-air-property calculations observed settling measurements.

DEFINITION AND EQUATION
The dynamic shape factor chi is the drag of the nonspherical particle
divided by the drag of a sphere of equal volume moving at the same speed,
with consistent flow-regime and orientation conventions. Chi is a SHAPE
factor; diameter separately represents size. Ideal spheres have chi = 1.
Larger chi means greater drag and slower settling at fixed volume/density.
It is not the Corey shape factor, sphericity, or a generic drag coefficient;
numerical values of those quantities cannot be substituted for chi.

The implemented approximate low-Reynolds-number settling expression is:
  v = (rho_particle - rho_air) * g * d_ve^2 * Cc(d_ve) / (18 * mu * chi)
  Cc = 1 + (2*lambda/d_ve) * [1.257 + 0.4*exp(-0.55*d_ve/lambda)]
where d_ve is volume-equivalent sphere diameter in meters. The buoyancy
correction is included. Velocity is exported in m/s and plotted in mm/s.
Reynolds number is rho_air*v*d_ve/mu.

Constants, held fixed rather than taken from hourly meteorology:
  gravitational acceleration g = 9.80616 m/s2
  air dynamic viscosity mu = 1.81e-5 Pa s
  air density rho_air = 1.2 kg/m3
  air mean free path lambda = 65e-9 m
These approximate near-ambient air properties do not describe every hour
of the 2023 meteorology. The constants and spherical slip expression match
the verified AERMOD26135 particle-settling implementation used as reference.

The correction matters for the smallest size: Cc is approximately 1.328
at 0.5 micrometers. Applying a sphere's slip correction at d_ve and then
dividing by a constant continuum chi is a schematic approximation for
nonspherical particles. Real effective chi can depend on size/Knudsen
number and orientation. These figures do not establish measured fiber or
film settling speeds, and they do not alter shape in the AERMOD runs.

SINGLE-PANEL theoretical_settling_v2
The first type of panel from the original theoretical_settling figure is
retained: settling velocity versus diameter. The Reynolds-number panel
is removed. Diameter range is 0.5-10 micrometers; the sphere curves mark
0.5, 1, 5, and 10 micrometers. Density colors represent 900, 1050, and
1400 kg/m3. Line styles represent chi = 1, 2, and 3. Values 2 and 3 are
explicit illustrative sensitivities, not assigned typical values for
fibers, fragments, films, or other microplastic morphotypes.
The largest Reynolds number in these plotted curves is approximately
0.00284; the low-Reynolds-number Stokes approximation is appropriate for
the spherical reference within this restricted size range.

SIMPLE settling_velocity_vs_chi
Volume-equivalent diameter is fixed at 10 micrometers and material density
at 1050 kg/m3. The curve varies chi only. Thus v(chi)/v(1) = 1/chi.
For chi = 1, 2, and 3, velocities are approximately 3.208, 1.604, and
1.069 mm/s. These are predictions for the stated equivalent-volume
surrogate, not observed velocities of particular morphotypes.
Literature annotations identify their reference geometry and source.
They must not be interpreted as universal microplastic morphotype means.

MORPHOTYPE EVIDENCE AND CAVEATS
The numerical source ledger is morphotype_shape_factors.json; the detailed
research record is literature_notes.md. These accompany the figure.
Published aerosol reference-shape values can support idealized proxies,
while published microplastic studies emphasize aspect ratio, orientation,
and flow-regime dependence. An ideal bead may be represented by a sphere;
a compact fragment by a cube proxy; a fiber by a specified cylinder proxy.
Irregular real fragments and fibers can differ greatly from those proxies.
No unsupported chi constant is imputed for film or foam. Hollow/porous
particles also require a clearly defined material/effective density and
equivalent-volume convention, beyond naming a morphotype.

Film                                  unassigned

The table is verified in Particula's Hinds-cited implementation, pinned at
commit acf38253e09e8f629bd5e2e93f3aa3a875e5cbd6. Hinds' original textbook
table was not independently accessed. It supplies secondary support for
reference geometries, not empirically typical environmental microplastic
values. The targeted studies inspected did not establish a defensible
typical chi per morphotype; this is not proof that no further data exist.
Exact source URL and retrieval hashes are supplied in the source ledger.

In particular, measured fiber-to-sphere velocity ratios from large,
finite-Reynolds-number microplastic fibers cannot simply be inverted and
used as micron-scale dynamic chi values. Values measured in water or
reported as Corey/Aschenbrenner shape factors are not substituted here.
The research ledger gives actual microplastic study dimensions and
conditions wherever relevant, alongside the chosen reference proxies.

Relevant atmospheric microplastic studies include Tatsii et al. (2024),
Shape Matters, doi:10.1021/acs.est.3c08209 (full text inspected), and
Preston et al. (2023), Effects of Shape and Size on Microplastic Atmospheric
Settling Velocity, doi:10.1021/acs.est.3c03671 (abstract inspected; full text
unavailable through the accessed sources). Literature_notes.md records
the evidence and access limits for these and the other studies checked.
